🎯 1. Lesson Overview & Objectives

Topic: Numerical and geometric patterns, inductive reasoning, conjectures, and counterexamples.

Learning objectives: Students will recognize patterns, extend sequences, formulate conjectures, find counterexamples, and explain why examples alone do not establish proof.

🙋 I CAN statements

  • I can recognize number and shape patterns.
  • I can predict terms and explain my rule.
  • I can write a mathematical conjecture.
  • I can disprove a universal conjecture with a counterexample.

❓ Essential question

How can recognizing patterns help us make predictions, and how can we determine whether those predictions are always true?

Guiding questions: What is inductive reasoning? What is a counterexample? Why is a proof different from several examples?

📘 2. Standards & Mathematical Practices

West Virginia Mathematical Habits of Mind: MHM1 (problem solving), MHM2 (abstract and quantitative reasoning), MHM3 (arguments and critique), MHM7 (structure), and MHM8 (regularity in repeated reasoning).

Geometry connection: Builds reasoning skills needed for geometric arguments, conditional statements, and proofs.

📚 3. Vocabulary, Prerequisites & Materials

TermStudent-friendly meaning
🔍 PatternAn arrangement that follows a rule
🔢 SequenceAn ordered list of numbers or objects
🧠 Inductive reasoningUsing specific observations to make a general prediction
💭 ConjectureA statement believed true based on observations
❌ CounterexampleAn example that proves a universal conjecture false
✅ ProofA logical argument showing why a statement must be true

🧩 Prerequisite skills

Basic arithmetic, simple sequences, familiar geometric figures, and verbal or written explanation of reasoning.

💻 Materials & platforms

myAMP, Canvas, Microsoft Teams, Nearpod, digital whiteboard, student notes, and teacher-created examples.

🌎 4. Real-World Relevance

🐝 Nature: Honeycomb hexagons and plant spirals
🧱 Construction: Brickwork and repeating tile layouts
💻 Technology: Recognizing regularities in data and algorithms
🏀 Sports: Observing trends without assuming they guarantee future outcomes

Big idea: Patterns help generate predictions, but a pattern observed in several cases is not automatically a proof.

🔥 5. Bell Ringer — Notice & Wonder (5 min)

Display the sequence and ask students to identify a rule, predict the next term, and explain their reasoning.

2, 4, 6, 8, 10, ___

Prompts: What do you notice? What comes next? Could a different rule fit the terms shown?

👀 Expected response & teaching point

Adding 2 suggests 12. A finite sequence alone does not uniquely determine the next term without a specified rule.

Formative check: Nearpod poll, Teams chat, or verbal response.

👨‍🏫 6. I DO — Explicit Teacher Modeling (12 min)

Example A • Arithmetic
3, 6, 9, 12, 15, ___

Think aloud: Each term increases by 3. If that rule continues, the next term is 18.

Example B • Multiplicative
2, 4, 8, 16, 32, ___

Think aloud: Multiply each term by 2. Next term: 64.

Example C • Visual geometric pattern
■
Figure 1 • 1 square
■■
■■
Figure 2 • 4 squares
■■■
■■■
■■■
Figure 3 • 9 squares
4 × 4
Figure 4 • 16 squares

Conjecture: Figure n has n² small squares, provided the square-array rule continues.

Example D • Counterexample

Conjecture: “All prime numbers are odd.” Although 3, 5, and 7 support the claim, 2 is a prime number and is even. Therefore, the conjecture is false.

Teacher emphasis: One valid counterexample disproves a universal statement; repeated examples alone do not prove it.

🤝 7. WE DO — Guided Practice (10 min)

Work collaboratively. Ask students to justify each answer using the relevant pattern or geometric property.

  1. 4, 8, 12, 16, ___
  2. 2, 4, 8, 16, ___
  3. Disprove: “All rectangles are squares.”
  4. How many counterexamples are needed to disprove a universal conjecture?
🔐 Guided practice answer key
  1. 20 (add 4)
  2. 32 (multiply by 2)
  3. A 3-by-5 rectangle is not a square.
  4. One valid counterexample.

Engagement: Nearpod polls, verbal explanations, Teams chat, and shared whiteboard. Teacher checks for misconceptions and models corrections.

✏️ 8. YOU DO — Independent Application (10 min)

  1. Extend 5, 10, 15, 20, ... by three terms.
  2. Extend 1, 4, 9, 16, ... by three terms.
  3. Write a conjecture about the sum of two even integers.
  4. Find a counterexample to “Every integer greater than 1 is prime.”
  5. Write an original conjecture about a geometric figure and explain the observations supporting it.
🔐 Suggested solutions
  1. 25, 30, 35
  2. 25, 36, 49
  3. The sum of two even integers is even.
  4. 4 is greater than 1 but is composite.
  5. Responses vary; assess mathematical clarity and supporting reasoning.

Teacher role: Monitor independent responses and provide individualized feedback.

🎮 9. Pattern Detective — Interactive Check (5 min)

Students predict the next term and explain the rule. This optional on-page check is for engagement; it does not submit grades to Canvas.

7, 14, 21, 28, ___

📝 Noninteractive pattern answers

7, 14, 21, 28 → 35 (add 7).
3, 6, 12, 24 → 48 (multiply by 2).
1, 8, 27, 64 → 125 (perfect cubes).

🎟️ 10. Closure & Exit Ticket (3 min)

  1. What is a conjecture?
  2. What is a counterexample?
  3. Give a counterexample to “All quadrilaterals are rectangles.”
🔐 Exit ticket answer guide
  1. A statement believed true based on observations.
  2. An example that disproves a conjecture.
  3. A nonrectangular trapezoid, for example.

Exit-ticket criterion: At least 2 of 3 correct, including a valid counterexample.

📊 11. Formative & Summative Assessment

During instruction: Bell ringer, guided responses, Nearpod polls, verbal reasoning, chat responses, and teacher observation.

After instruction: Independent practice and exit ticket; optional follow-up Canvas assessment.

Mastery target: At least 80% accuracy on independent or follow-up assessment. Review reasoning as well as correct answers.

Teacher look-fors: Students identify rules, justify predictions, form conjectures, and use genuine counterexamples.

🤝 12. Differentiation, Accommodations & Support

🪜 Additional support

  • Partially completed sequences
  • Color-coded differences or ratios
  • Visual diagrams and guided notes
  • Sentence frame: “I predict ___ because ___.”

♿ IEP / 504 implementation

  • Follow each student's documented accommodations
  • Provide additional processing time as required
  • Repeat directions and check understanding
  • Allow approved response formats

🔁 Reteaching

  • Small-group targeted instruction
  • Model simpler arithmetic patterns
  • Practice valid versus invalid counterexamples
  • Reassess after feedback

🚀 Enrichment

  • Create and test original conjectures
  • Compare inductive reasoning with proof
  • Show algebraically why two odd integers sum to an even integer

📝 13. Assignment & Future Learning

Assignment: Complete assigned myAMP or Canvas practice on recognizing patterns, conjectures, and counterexamples. Students explain their reasoning and follow established submission expectations.

Connection forward: Prepares students for conditional statements, deductive reasoning, and formal geometric proofs in Module 2.
